After logging into **wholewellnesscoaching.org** with the Coach Chuck test credentials, I conducted another deep dive across the site’s pages and functions. Below is an updated, candid review of the current state of the platform, highlighting what works, what doesn’t, and forward‑focused suggestions to help the service shine.

---

## Overall Feel

The site’s mission—to offer compassionate coaching for marginalized communities—is front and center. The **About Us** page introduces co‑founders Dr. Charlene Inman Smith and Dasha Lazaryuk with inspiring bios and portraits. Further down, the page outlines core values of **Accessibility**, **Empowerment**, **Compassion** and **Transformation**, then boasts metrics like **500 + lives transformed, 75 % of clients receiving free or discounted services, and a 95 % satisfaction rate**. A “Looking Forward” section describes plans to expand reach and make coaching accessible to all. This narrative builds trust and positions WholeWellness as a mission‑driven organization.

Despite the strong vision, the practical experience is inconsistent. I encountered many broken links, unfinished features and confusing routing, which undermine user confidence and make the platform feel like a beautiful house with doors that don’t open.

---

## Digital Onboarding & Assessments

The discovery quiz still guides users through selecting support areas, describing their situation and choosing a coaching plan. However:

* The **progress indicator bug** (“Step 5 of 4”) remains. Finishing the quiz shows recommended coaches but no confirmation or saved record of the results, leaving users unsure what to do next.
* The **Wellness Personality Assessment** still fails on submission; after completing the two‑step form, I received a backend error complaining about a null `user_id`. Without results or feedback, it’s impossible to gauge progress.
* On the **Mental Wellness** hub, the big buttons for **Quick Assessment**, **Browse Resources** and **Crisis Support** change colour when clicked yet do nothing. Likewise, the “Access Resource” buttons for NAMI, Psychology Today, Calm, Headspace and other mental‑health partners produce no navigation or content.

### Recommendations

* Fix the quiz progress bug and ensure results are stored in the user’s dashboard.
* Repair the assessment submission endpoint, passing the logged‑in `user_id` and returning a clear results page.
* Make the Quick Assessment and Crisis Support buttons open relevant pages or modals; link each “Access Resource” button directly to the partner’s site.

---

## AI Chat & Coaching Packages

The **AI Coaching** page offers several AI coaches (mental health, mindfulness, relationship, etc.). When I clicked “Start Chat,” the chat opened with a paywall banner encouraging a $19.99/month subscription. Sending a message produced a polite fallback: “To get personalized AI coaching responses, please sign in … our AI coaches are designed to provide thoughtful, evidence‑based guidance tailored to your unique needs”. At least this time the bot didn’t hang or endlessly type; it clearly explained the limitation. However, the call‑to‑action still feels deceptive because the site invites free chat but the model only responds with generic text.

### Suggestions

* Clarify up front that personalized AI coaching requires a subscription. Remove the impression of a free trial if none exists.
* Provide a sample session or limited free messages to let users experience the service before paying.
* Ensure AI responses are relevant and empathetic; avoid generic fallback messages where possible.

---

## Wellness Journey

The **My Wellness Journey** page encourages users to “Begin My Wellness Journey,” yet clicking the button merely triggers a toast saying “Journey Created” while nothing else happens. There is no journey content, plan or next steps.

### Suggestions

* Either implement the journey feature or temporarily remove the button to avoid user frustration.
* If a journey plan is created, send a summary to the user’s dashboard or email with actionable steps, resources and progress tracking.

---

## Resource Library & Mental Wellness Tools

The **Resource Library** lists various guides, audios and worksheets, but clicking “Access” still does nothing. The **Mental Wellness** hub contains high‑quality organizations like NAMI, BetterHelp and SAMHSA, yet links aren’t wired up. Users expect these to open external resources.

### Suggestions

* Activate all resource links or remove placeholders until content is available.
* For partner resources, consider opening in a new tab with a disclaimer to help maintain trust.
* Provide filtering/search functions that actually work; the category and type selectors currently have no effect.

---

## Coach Tools & Professional Development

The **Certification Courses** page requires authentication and shows an “Authentication Required” error for regular users. The **Become a Coach** page is accessible and outlines reasons to join, but the multi‑step application form is long and appears to lead to a 7‑step process (I did not complete it). Because the login status persists and the **Logout** button still fails, it’s difficult to test these flows properly.

### Suggestions

* Fix the logout bug so users can properly switch roles or test features as a coach.
* Clarify who can view certification courses and how to gain access; consider free teaser content or sample lessons.
* Simplify the coach application process by allowing applicants to save progress and return later.

---

## Contact, Appointment Booking & Donations

The **Contact Us** page provides multiple pathways—schedule consultation, general inquiries, crisis support, community support—with well‑structured forms. The booking page displays services with prices and a 4‑step booking workflow. I did not complete any bookings or donations to avoid unintended transactions, but the flow appears functional. The **Donate** page offers a quick‑donation form with custom amount and frequency fields; however, it defaults to “Donate $0,” which might be confusing.

### Suggestions

* Change the default donation button to “Enter Amount” or automatically update the button label when the user inputs a number.
* Provide clear details about where donations go and how they impact the mission.
* For the booking flow, add a summary/confirmation before final submission and provide a cancellation policy.

---

## About Us and Vision

The **About Us** page is well‑constructed. It humanizes the organization by profiling the founders and explaining the mission. The “Our Approach” section highlights the pillars of accessibility, empowerment, compassion and transformation. A “Looking Forward” segment lays out aspirations to expand reach, innovate and make coaching a fundamental right. This storytelling fosters connection and trust—keep it.

---

## Final Thoughts

WholeWellness Coaching blends a noble mission with an appealing aesthetic. Yet numerous unfinished features, broken links and misrouted buttons hamper its credibility. To attract and retain users, the platform must focus on reliability: ensure assessments return results, link buttons to their destinations, provide clear guidance for AI coaching subscriptions, and fix the persistent logout bug. Once these foundational issues are resolved, WholeWellness can focus on scaling its impact and delivering the accessible, compassionate support it promises.
